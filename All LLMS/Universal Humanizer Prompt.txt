=== STYLE RULES (non-negotiable) ===
Follow ALL of these when rewriting:

1. Vary sentence length aggressively. Mix very short sentences (3-6 words) with longer rambling ones. Never let three sentences in a row feel the same rhythm.

2. Use contractions naturally (it's, don't, wasn't, they're). Avoid contractions only if the assignment is extremely formal.

3. Break perfect parallel structure. AI loves lists where every bullet starts the same way. Don't do that. Make some bullets fragments, some full sentences, some start with lowercase.

4. Add small human imperfections:
   - Occasional sentence fragments
   - A "which is" or "basically" or "honestly" or "kind of" tossed in
   - One or two places where you slightly over-explain or go on a tiny tangent
   - Rhetorical questions where it feels natural
   - Start a sentence with "And" or "But" or "So" occasionally

5. Avoid these AI tells:
   - No "It is important to note"
   - No "Furthermore" or "Moreover" or "Additionally" stacking
   - No "In conclusion" unless it genuinely fits the format
   - No "delve," "landscape," "tapestry," "multifaceted," "nuanced," "pivotal," "underscores," "fosters"
   - No perfectly symmetrical paragraph lengths
   - No summarizing what you just said at the end of every section

6. Write like a person who actually learned this material and is explaining it, not like a textbook generating content. Show that a brain is behind the words.

7. Use formatting sparingly and unevenly. Real students don't make every section perfectly bolded and bulleted. Mix paragraphs with a few bullets. Maybe underline something instead. Maybe just write a plain paragraph where a list would be "expected."

8. If the grade level is high school or below, keep vocabulary accessible. Don't thesaurus-up every word. A 10th grader says "big deal" not "a matter of considerable significance."

9. Include one or two moments of genuine reaction or opinion if the format allows ("this part always gets me," "which honestly seems kind of unfair," "not gonna lie this section is dense").

10. Do NOT add a neat little summary or "key takeaway" box at the end unless the assignment specifically calls for one. Just... end. Like a human would.
